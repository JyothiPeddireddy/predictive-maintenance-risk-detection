## How to Interpret These Results

- **Synthetic data only:** Failures were injected by the project's generator. The risk-scoring rules were designed to recognise patterns created by that generator, such as rising levels, trends and bursts. Real industrial data may be more complex, and real-world performance may be lower. Validation on real industrial data has not been performed.
- **Limited holdout independence:** Holdout seeds create new random datasets, but they use the same generator and failure mechanisms. They help evaluate performance on unseen generated datasets, but do not prove that the system will generalise to real machines.
- **Strict labels:** A warning more than 72 hours before a failure is counted as a false positive in precision and recall, even if the machine was genuinely degrading. These cases are reported separately as earlier warnings.
- **Lead-time limit:** Warning lead time is capped at 72 hours because the evaluation uses a 72-hour prediction horizon.
- **Correlated observations:** Risk scores are calculated every three hours. Consecutive scoring times are strongly correlated, so precision and recall should not be treated as independent observations.
- **Event recall:** Event recall is a useful measure because it shows how many actual failure events were detected. However, the number of failure events is relatively small, so uncertainty remains high.

These results demonstrate performance on controlled synthetic datasets. Real-world reliability must be established through validation with actual industrial telemetry and maintenance records.
